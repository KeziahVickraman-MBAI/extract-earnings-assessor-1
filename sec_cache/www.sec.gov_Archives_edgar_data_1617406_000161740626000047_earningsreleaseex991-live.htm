Park Hotels & Resorts Inc. Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
TYSONS, VA (November 4, 2026) – Park Hotels & Resorts Inc. (“Park” or the “Company”) (NYSE: PK) today announced results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026 and provided an operational update and an update on its Non-Core hotel disposition initiative.
Third Quarter Highlights Include:
•Comparable RevPAR was $201.02, an increase of 8.4% compared to the same period in 2025;
•Core RevPAR was $213.66, an increase of 8.1% compared to the same period in 2025;
•Net loss and net loss attributable to stockholders were $(15) million and $(18) million, respectively;
•Adjusted EBITDA was $135 million, an increase of 4.0% compared to the same period in 2025;
•Diluted loss per share was $(0.10); and
•Diluted Adjusted FFO per share was $0.39, an increase of 11.2% compared to the same period in 2025.

Thomas J. Baltimore, Jr., Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, stated, “I am very pleased with our third quarter results, with Core RevPAR increasing over 8% year-over-year, driven by continued strength in both group and leisure demand segments across our portfolio. Group rooms revenue increased over 18% year-over-year, while higher-rated leisure travel further supported performance during the quarter. Our Hawaii hotels delivered outstanding results, with RevPAR at the Hilton Waikoloa Village and Hilton Hawaiian Village increasing 20% and 5% year-over-year, respectively, despite a nearly 280-basis point impact on combined RevPAR growth from recent storms. We also continued to realize the benefits of our strategic capital investments, with the Casa Marina Key West, Curio Collection, and Waldorf Astoria Orlando generating RevPAR growth of over 17% and 16% year-over-year, respectively, while the Hilton Chicago, Hilton Santa Barbara Beachfront Resort and each of our Washington, D.C. and Boston hotels produced double-digit RevPAR growth, further demonstrating the broad-based demand strength within the portfolio. As we enter the fourth quarter, booking trends remain encouraging as preliminary October Comparable RevPAR increased nearly 11% year-over-year, with continued outperformance throughout the portfolio contributing to our raised outlook for the remainder of the year.”
Additional Highlights Include:
•Reopened the Royal Palm South Beach Miami, a Tribute Portfolio Resort (“Royal Palm”), in July 2026, following the completion of its more than $110 million transformative renovation, and commenced a more than $100 million renovation of the Ali’i Tower at the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort in August 2026;
•Exited two Non-Core hotels since the second quarter of 2026 for gross proceeds of approximately $31 million. The hotels contributed an insignificant amount of Hotel Adjusted EBITDA in 2025 and had approximately $80 million in anticipated capital expenditures;
•In September 2026, drew the remaining $600 million from Park’s $800 million senior unsecured delayed draw term loan facility (“2025 Delayed Draw Term Loan”), as well as $700 million from Park’s delayed draw mortgage loan facility (“Bonnet Creek Mortgage Loan”), to fully repay the $1.275 billion CMBS loan secured by the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort (“HHV Mortgage Loan”), which was scheduled to mature on November 1, 2026;
•In October 2026, paid its third quarter cash dividend of $0.25 per share to stockholders of record as of September 30, 2026 and declared its fourth quarter cash dividend of $0.25 per share to stockholders of record as of December 31, 2026, to be paid on January 15, 2027; and
•Achieved LEED Silver Certifications for the 822-room Rainbow Tower at the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort and the 414-room Palace Tower at the Hilton Waikoloa Village, both of which completed guestroom renovations earlier this year. Including the 1,021-room Tapa Tower at the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort, which earned a hotel LEED certification in 2025, Park has three towers with LEED certifications following comprehensive renovations.

Non-Core Hotel Dispositions:
•In July 2026, sold the 314-room Hilton Short Hills for gross proceeds of $12 million, which had anticipated capital expenditures of approximately $27 million; and
•In October 2026, sold the 520-room The Wade for gross proceeds of approximately $19 million, which had anticipated capital expenditures of approximately $52 million.

Mr. Baltimore added, “We are excited about the reopening of the Royal Palm in South Beach following a comprehensive renovation that transformed all 393 existing guestrooms, added 11 new guestrooms, expanded and modernized the meeting space, and enhanced all public spaces, including a redesigned lobby, four new food and beverage concepts and an upgraded pool. We expect the renovation will be a positive tailwind for the portfolio next year, adding at least 200 basis points to Comparable RevPAR growth and doubling the hotel’s EBITDA upon stabilization. We remain laser-focused on further elevating the quality of our portfolio and, during the quarter, commenced the more than $100 million renovation of the Ali’i Tower at the Hilton Hawaiian Village, as part of our continued investment in this iconic resort. We also made meaningful progress in strengthening our financial flexibility by repaying the $1.275 billion mortgage loan secured by the Hilton Hawaiian Village and extending our debt maturity profile through utilization of proceeds from our 2025 Delayed Draw Term Loan and Bonnet Creek Mortgage Loan. In addition, we continued to advance our portfolio transformation through the sale of two additional Non-Core hotels. With approximately $1.3 billion of liquidity, we are well-positioned to continue executing on our strategic priorities and creating long-term shareholder value.”

During the third quarter of 2026, Park's resort hotels continued to drive the performance of its portfolio, including the Royal Palm in Miami, which reopened in July 2026 after suspending operations in mid-May 2025 for a comprehensive renovation. Despite the impact of recent storms, RevPAR at the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort increased 5%, while RevPAR at the Hilton Waikoloa Village increased over 20% driven by a nearly 40% increase in group revenue and an over 20% increase in transient revenue. In addition, the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort benefited from the completion of guestroom renovations at the Rainbow Tower earlier in the year, helping to drive an over 130% increase in group revenue and a nearly 35%, or over $6 million, increase in food and beverage revenue compared to the same period in 2025. The Casa Marina Key West, Curio Collection, benefited from increases in group and transient revenue of 60% and nearly 15%, respectively, during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, resulting in an increase in RevPAR of over 17%. The Waldorf Astoria Orlando continued to benefit from the comprehensive renovation and expansion projects completed in early 2024, with transient revenue increasing over 25% during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, which increased RevPAR by over 16%. The Hilton Santa Barbara Beachfront Resort benefited from increases in group and transient revenue of approximately 25% and 10%, respectively, during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, driving a 13% increase in RevPAR.

Additionally, the Hilton Chicago benefited from increases in both group and transient demand during the third quarter of 2026, resulting in an over 15% increase in RevPAR, while food and beverage revenue increased nearly 20%, or nearly $3 million, compared to the same period in 2025. Park's hotels in Washington, D.C. benefited from government-related demand, which drove an over 160% increase in group revenue during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025 and increased combined RevPAR by nearly 33%. Park's Boston hotels also benefited from increased group and transient demand during the quarter from high-profile events, including the World Cup quarterfinal match, resulting in a nearly 13% increase in combined RevPAR.
3

While higher demand and occupancy drove the 8% increase in Comparable RevPAR during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, hotel operating expenses increased 5%, primarily reflecting higher variable costs associated with increased occupancy, as well as wage and utility pressures. Additionally, the increase in group demand primarily benefited rooms revenue, with lower out-of-room spend than Park typically recognizes from in-house groups, which resulted in only a 2% increase in food and beverage revenue, while banquet and catering revenue remained relatively flat compared to the same period in 2025. Park also recognized lower cancellation and attrition fees during the quarter.
At the end of September 2026, Core Group Revenue Pace for 2027 increased nearly 6%, as compared to what bookings were for 2026 at the end of September 2025, with average Core group rates for 2027 projected to increase nearly 4% for the same time period.

Balance Sheet and Liquidity
As of September 30, 2026, Park’s liquidity was approximately $1.3 billion, including $1 billion of available capacity under the senior unsecured revolving credit facility (“Revolver”).
In September 2026, Park drew the remaining $600 million from the 2025 Delayed Draw Term Loan and $700 million from the Bonnet Creek Mortgage Loan to fully repay the $1.275 billion HHV Mortgage Loan, which was scheduled to mature on November 1, 2026, with the remaining proceeds to be used for general corporate purposes. The Bonnet Creek Mortgage Loan is secured by the 1,009-room Signia by Hilton Orlando Bonnet Creek and the 502-room Waldorf Astoria Orlando and associated golf course (collectively, the “Bonnet Creek complex”). Park also expects to refinance the $150 million mortgage loan encumbering the Hilton Santa Barbara Beachfront Resort in mid-November 2026. As of September 30, 2026, Park’s Net Debt was approximately $3.7 billion, and the weighted average maturity of Park’s consolidated debt is 3.1 years, including all extension options.

Capital Investments
During the third quarter of 2026, Park spent $57 million on capital improvements at its hotels and expects to spend between $270 million to $280 million in capital expenditures during 2026.
Park reopened the Royal Palm in July 2026, following the completion of its more than $110 million comprehensive renovation, which began in May 2025. Park expects the renovation to generate a 15% to 20% return on investment. Park also expects to complete the third and final phase of guestroom renovations at the main tower of the Hilton New Orleans Riverside during the fourth quarter of 2026, bringing the total investment across all three phases to approximately $80 million.

Additionally, in August 2026, Park commenced a more than $100 million renovation of the 348-room Ali’i Tower at the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort, including the addition of three new guestrooms at the oceanfront tower.

Dividends
Park declared a third quarter 2026 cash dividend of $0.25 per share to stockholders of record as of September 30, 2026. The third quarter dividend was paid on October 15, 2026.

On October 29, 2026, Park declared a fourth quarter 2026 cash dividend of $0.25 per share to be paid on January 15, 2027 to stockholders of record as of December 31, 2026. The declared dividends translate to an annualized yield of approximately 6% based on Park’s recent trading levels.

DEFINITIONS
Comparable
The Company presents certain data for its consolidated hotels on a Comparable basis as supplemental information for investors: Comparable Hotel Revenues, Comparable RevPAR, Comparable Occupancy, Comparable ADR, Comparable Hotel Adjusted EBITDA and Comparable Hotel Adjusted EBITDA Margin. The Company presents Comparable hotel results to help the Company and its investors evaluate the ongoing operating performance of its hotels. The Company’s Comparable hotel financial data includes results from Park’s consolidated hotels and property acquisitions as though such acquisitions occurred on the earliest period presented. Additionally, Comparable hotel financial data excludes results from property dispositions that have occurred prior to November 4, 2026.
Core/Non-Core
The Company’s Core portfolio includes 20 of Park’s consolidated hotels and one unconsolidated hotel and consists primarily of hotels and resorts that cater to group and leisure demand. As of September 30, 2026, Park’s Non-Core portfolio included 9 consolidated hotels. As of November 4, 2026, Park had 8 hotels remaining in its Non-Core portfolio. Financial data presented for Park’s Core and Non-Core hotels are based on its consolidated hotels only.
EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Hotel Adjusted EBITDA and Hotel Adjusted EBITDA margin
Earnings before interest expense, taxes and depreciation and amortization (“EBITDA”), presented herein, reflects net income (loss) excluding depreciation and amortization, interest income, interest expense, income taxes and also interest income and expense, income tax and depreciation and amortization included in equity in earnings from investments in affiliates.
Adjusted EBITDA, presented herein, is calculated as EBITDA, as previously defined, further adjusted to exclude the following items that are not reflective of Park’s ongoing operating performance or incurred in the normal course of business, and thus, excluded from management’s analysis in making day-to-day operating decisions and evaluations of Park’s operating performance against other companies within its industry:
•Gains or losses on sales of assets for both consolidated and unconsolidated investments;
•Costs associated with hotel acquisitions or dispositions expensed during the period;
•Severance expense;
•Share-based compensation expense;
•Impairment losses and casualty gains or losses; and
•Other items that management believes are not representative of the Company’s current or future operating performance.
Hotel Adjusted EBITDA measures hotel-level results before debt service, depreciation and corporate expenses of the Company’s consolidated hotels, which excludes hotels owned by unconsolidated affiliates, and is a key measure of the Company’s profitability. The Company presents Hotel Adjusted EBITDA to help the Company and its investors evaluate the ongoing operating performance of the Company’s consolidated hotels.
Hotel Adjusted EBITDA margin is calculated as Hotel Adjusted EBITDA divided by total hotel revenue.
EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Hotel Adjusted EBITDA and Hotel Adjusted EBITDA margin are not recognized terms under United States (“U.S.”) GAAP and should not be considered as alternatives to net income (loss) or other measures of financial performance or liquidity derived in accordance with U.S. GAAP. In addition, the Company’s definitions of EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Hotel Adjusted EBITDA and Hotel Adjusted EBITDA margin may not be comparable to similarly titled measures of other companies.
The Company believes that EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Hotel Adjusted EBITDA and Hotel Adjusted EBITDA margin provide useful information to investors about the Company and its financial condition and results of operations for the following reasons: (i) EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Hotel Adjusted EBITDA and Hotel Adjusted EBITDA margin are among the measures used by the Company’s

EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Hotel Adjusted EBITDA and Hotel Adjusted EBITDA margin have limitations as analytical tools and should not be considered either in isolation or as a substitute for net income (loss) or other methods of analyzing the Company’s operating performance and results as reported under U.S. GAAP. Nareit FFO attributable to stockholders, Adjusted FFO attributable to stockholders, Nareit FFO per share – diluted and Adjusted FFO per share – diluted
Nareit FFO attributable to stockholders and Nareit FFO per diluted share (defined as set forth below) are presented herein as non-GAAP measures of the Company’s performance. The Company calculates funds from (used in) operations (“FFO”) attributable to stockholders for a given operating period in accordance with standards established by the National Association of Real Estate Investment Trusts (“Nareit”), as net income (loss) attributable to stockholders (calculated in accordance with U.S. GAAP), excluding depreciation and amortization, gains or losses on sales of assets, impairment, and the cumulative effect of changes in accounting principles, plus adjustments for unconsolidated joint ventures. Adjustments for unconsolidated joint ventures are calculated to reflect the Company’s pro rata share of the FFO of those entities on the same basis. As noted by Nareit in its December 2018 “Nareit Funds from Operations White Paper – 2018 Restatement,” since real estate values historically have risen or fallen with market conditions, many industry investors have considered presentation of operating results for real estate companies that use historical cost accounting to be insufficient by themselves. For these reasons, Nareit adopted the FFO metric in order to promote an industry-wide measure of REIT operating performance. The Company believes Nareit FFO provides useful information to investors regarding its operating performance and can facilitate comparisons of operating performance between periods and between REITs. The Company’s presentation may not be comparable to FFO reported by other REITs that do not define the terms in accordance with the current Nareit definition, or that interpret the current Nareit definition differently. The Company calculates Nareit FFO per diluted share as Nareit FFO divided by the number of fully diluted shares outstanding during a given operating period.
The Company also presents Adjusted FFO attributable to stockholders and Adjusted FFO per diluted share when evaluating its performance because management believes that the exclusion of certain additional items described below provides useful supplemental information to investors regarding the Company’s ongoing operating performance. Management historically has made the adjustments detailed below in evaluating its performance and in its annual budget process. Management believes that the presentation of Adjusted FFO provides useful supplemental information that is beneficial to an investor’s complete understanding of operating performance. The Company adjusts Nareit FFO attributable to stockholders for the following items, which may occur in any period, and refers to this measure as Adjusted FFO attributable to stockholders:
•Costs associated with hotel acquisitions or dispositions expensed during the period;
•Severance expense;
•Share-based compensation expense;
•Casualty gains or losses; and
•Other items that management believes are not representative of the Company’s current or future operating performance.

Net Debt
Net Debt, presented herein, is a non-GAAP financial measure that the Company uses to evaluate its financial leverage. Net Debt is calculated as (i) debt excluding unamortized deferred financing costs; and (ii) the Company’s share of investments in affiliate debt, excluding unamortized deferred financing costs; reduced by (a) cash and cash equivalents; and (b) restricted cash and cash equivalents.
The Company believes Net Debt provides useful information about its indebtedness to investors as it is frequently used by securities analysts, investors and other interested parties to compare the indebtedness of companies. Net Debt should not be considered as a substitute to debt presented in accordance with U.S. GAAP. Net Debt may not be comparable to a similarly titled measure of other companies.
Occupancy
Occupancy represents the total number of room nights sold divided by the total number of room nights available at a hotel or group of hotels. Occupancy measures the utilization of the Company’s hotels’ available capacity. Management uses Occupancy to gauge demand at a specific hotel or group of hotels in a given period. Occupancy levels also help management determine achievable Average Daily Rate (“ADR”) levels as demand for rooms increases or decreases.
Average Daily Rate
ADR (or rate) represents rooms revenue divided by total number of room nights sold in a given period. ADR measures average room price attained by a hotel and ADR trends provide useful information concerning the pricing environment and the nature of the customer base of a hotel or group of hotels. ADR is a commonly used performance measure in the hotel industry, and management uses ADR to assess pricing levels that the Company is able to generate by type of customer, as changes in rates have a more pronounced effect on overall revenues and incremental profitability than changes in Occupancy, as described above.
Revenue per Available Room
Revenue per Available Room (“RevPAR”) represents rooms revenue divided by the total number of room nights available to guests for a given period. Management considers RevPAR to be a meaningful indicator of the Company’s performance as it provides a metric correlated to two primary and key factors of operations at a hotel or group of hotels: Occupancy and ADR. RevPAR is also a useful indicator in measuring performance over comparable periods.
Total RevPAR
Total RevPAR represents rooms, food and beverage and other hotel revenues divided by the total number of room nights available to guests for a given period. Management considers Total RevPAR to be a meaningful indicator of the Company’s performance as approximately one-third of revenues are earned from food and beverage and other hotel revenues. Total RevPAR is also a useful indicator in measuring performance over comparable periods.
Group Revenue Pace
Group Revenue Pace represents bookings for future business and is calculated as group room nights multiplied by the contracted room rate expressed as a percentage of a prior period relative to a prior point in time.
